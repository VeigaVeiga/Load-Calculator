How to Review a Railway Container Loading Plan

A practical review starts with verified package dimensions, gross weights, quantity, orientation and stacking restrictions. Select the correct container type, compare the usable internal dimensions with the cargo envelope, and arrange heavy or dimensionally sensitive packages with suitable clearances. Review the 3D arrangement and the top, side and end views before sharing the proposed plan.

  1. Prepare a cargo list with length, width, height, quantity and unit weight.
  2. Confirm the container type, usable dimensions, door opening and payload limit from the actual equipment data.
  3. Arrange the cargo and check package clearances, access and stacking constraints.
  4. Review the combined centre of gravity and longitudinal and transverse weight distribution.
  5. Show proposed securing materials and have the final arrangement checked by qualified personnel.

How Cargo Centre of Gravity Is Calculated

For each coordinate axis, the combined cargo centre of gravity is the sum of each package's mass multiplied by its centre coordinate, divided by total cargo mass. For example, if a 1,200 kg package has its centre at 2.0 m and an 800 kg package has its centre at 6.0 m from the same reference point, the combined longitudinal coordinate is (1,200 × 2.0 + 800 × 6.0) ÷ 2,000 = 3.6 m. This is an illustrative calculation, not a loading limit or safety threshold.

Planning Scope and Operational Checks

A digital loading plan is a planning aid, not an approval certificate. Confirm actual container dimensions and payload, floor and concentrated-load limits, package handling requirements, securing-point capacity, wagon and route restrictions, and carrier instructions before loading. The software does not replace certified weighing, engineering calculations or railway acceptance.
